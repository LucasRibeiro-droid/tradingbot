## What it teaches

### 1. What equilibrium is (05:37–06:24)
- "A retracement tool to literally show you where big money is likely to buy again" (05:38–05:46).
- Price, like a grocery store: banks want a cheaper price to buy and a higher price to sell. "If they're looking to long they want a cheaper price; if they're looking to short they want a higher price" (05:52–06:24).

### 2. How it's measured (06:27–08:20)
- Measure "from the high to a low or the low to a high" and find "the 50 mark" (06:32–06:50).
- **Downtrend:** from the swing high to the swing low. When price retraces back up **above the 50 mark**, that's a good price to go short ("it's on sale"), because it's a high price (06:50–07:24).
- **Uptrend:** from the swing low to the swing high. As price retraces down, **anything below the 50 mark** "is going to be a discount" for the market movers to place more orders (07:55–08:20).
- "Finding the premium and the discount of a swing high to a swing low or a swing low to a swing high" (07:50–07:57). Premium = above 50%, discount = below 50% [TJR 11:16–11:22: "they wouldn't want to enter within a premium ... it's expensive"].

### 3. Not an entry on its own (08:20–09:01)
- "I like to use equilibrium not necessarily as an entry point, but ... as a gauge: this price range is where people would like to buy. Now let me find more of a reason to get in": an order block, a fair value gap, "or even just a break of structure while in a discount, that's enough reason for me to get in. I'm not the type of person to enter purely off equilibrium" (08:21–08:48).
- "It's the price range where you can find a discount for a re-entry" (10:57–11:06).

### 4. The toolkit, ranked again (09:05–10:12)
- "Liquidity sweep is essentially the **inducement**. The break of structure is our first primary entry" (09:08–09:16).
- Missed it? "Your first retracement entry point, which is an order block, the actual move that causes the liquidity sweep. If you see liquidity sweep, break of structure, tap into an order block, **reaction off of it, or even just hitting the order block, enter off that**" (09:18–09:33).
- Missed that too: the fair value gap, "we see it fill an imbalance and then we see a reaction off of it, enter" (09:40–09:49).
- "Equilibrium, which we can pair together with a fair value gap, which is honestly **the perfect tools to combine** for a retracement: a fair value gap within a discounted price, and then you get a reaction, let's enter" (09:49–10:10).

### 5. A live-trading remark (Discord ad, 13:55–14:27)
- "We get a break of structure, I don't want to enter here because it's **bad risk reward**. I think it'll come up and hit this order block, and that's where I'll want to enter ... if it hits the fair value gap and it gives us a reaction off of it I'll enter off that, but **if it hits the order block I'm instantly entering** when that price point gets hit."

## Connections
- **Days 14–24:** the last of the three retracement tools (order block, fair value gap, equilibrium), in the order Day 20 gave. Day 16 already said gaps and equilibrium are both continuation tools.
- **Day 18:** the "50%" of a gap was a take-profit level; equilibrium is a 50% of a whole swing.
- **Days 4, 6:** the swing is measured within the trend; in an uptrend only the discount half matters.
- **Days 8–12:** the sweep is now called the "inducement".
- **Days 22, 24 (contradiction):** there, the entry after price reaches an order block was a lower-timeframe break. Here, "even just hitting the order block, enter off that"; "if it hits the order block I'm instantly entering".
