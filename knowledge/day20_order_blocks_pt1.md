## What it teaches

### 1. What an order block is (02:41–04:01; frame at 03:40)
- A liquidity sweep needs a move: to take a high there must be a move up, to take a low a move down, before the break of structure that shifts the market (02:45–03:05).
- "All an order block is is that move up that causes the liquidity sweep ... when liquidity gets swept, orders get filled. That's why it's called an order block" (03:10–03:19).
- "This whole price range is where the orders were filled" (03:24–03:28). The frame shows a box drawn over the **whole up-leg** that took a high on gold, before the drop.
- "That move up or that move down taking out liquidity, where orders are being filled, is our order block" (03:54–04:01).

### 2. Why price comes back to it (04:01–06:09)
- The market makers filled their orders within that leg. Like fair value gaps, they want to push price back into that range "to fill more orders" and move the market further the same way (04:37–05:06).
- "It's the price range from where the first orders were filled, and then when we get that retracement up into the order block, that's where they can fill even more orders ... to send price further down in that same direction" (05:45–06:09).

### 3. Where it ranks among the tools (06:12–07:49)
- "Liquidity sweep and break of structure, that's the start of the move ... the top of the entry" (06:16–06:22).
- "Fair value gaps, order blocks and equilibrium are used for retracements": if you miss the sweep-and-break entry, you have "three more options for a re-entry, for a retracement play" (06:24–06:57).
- "The first option every single time for me is going to be an order block," because it's "still at the top of the move" and often gives "an even better entry than off of just the liquidity sweep and the break of structure", since the sweep's low is "the very beginning of the order block" (06:57–07:41).
- Then the fair value gap, then equilibrium; equilibrium and fair value gaps are "pretty similar" (07:44–07:49).

### 4. One per trend (07:50–09:35; frame at 09:12)
- "There's only one order block within a whole trend, because they form off of a liquidity sweep, orders getting filled, and then the start of a new trend" (07:53–08:09).
- Inside the new trend there are fair value gaps and equilibrium, but "no other order blocks"; "the next one that gets made is when we get another trend shift" (08:30–08:52).
- Example: the order block of the next shift "is the leg down prior to the liquidity sweep", then price goes higher (08:52–09:18).
- "Yes, there can be order blocks on every single time frame" (09:23).

### 5. Recap (09:45–10:18)
- It's "the move prior to the liquidity sweep that causes the liquidity sweep", and because orders filled there "we can get that break of structure". After the break, price "will likely want to go back up into that area, fill even more orders" and continue.
- They're "awesome" on lower timeframes and appear "literally every single day ... every single hour ... one-minute time frames" (10:45–10:57).

### 6. Order block = accumulation area (11:03–11:30)
- "I call them like accumulation areas ... an order block, accumulation area, all pretty much the same thing. Everyone loves giving different terminologies for different things just to sound fancy." This answers Day 18's "accumulation" (used there as a target).

### 7. Aside: news days (11:38–12:45)
- "Hopefully you guys did not trade today": PPI and FOMC "all through here". GBPJPY had moved only 34 pips since the US open. "This is why we need to know when news is going on." Day 19 (How to Read News Data, not received) presumably covers this.
