## What it teaches

### 1. Summary (07:16–08:55)
- "It's the leg up or the leg down prior to the break of structure to the upside or to the downside" (07:16–07:25).
- Why it matters: it's "the move that causes the liquidity sweep". The sweep fills the orders, then price breaks structure, "showing a market structure shift". When price comes back into that range, orders fill again and "price continues" (07:53–08:55).

### 2. How to box one (09:49–11:49): two ways
- "It's almost always best when we see a liquidity sweep as well" (09:52).
- **Beginners: box the entire leg.** "This is probably going to be your best bet ... there's no reason to get advanced with it, just box off the entire thing" (10:15–10:26). Repeated at the end: "don't try and be a pro at something that you're ass at ... if I'm you guys I'm using that big ass box and I'm marking out the entire leg down prior to the move up, or the entire leg up prior to the move down" (25:16–25:54).
- **TJR's own way: from the wick to the start of the body.** "I literally just do it off the wick of the candle ... I just box off the wick" (10:38–10:42). "Oftentimes it'll tap into just the wick area and then sometimes respond, or it'll tap just the base ... the base of the body, right where the body starts. Usually that's like the reaction point. I don't know why ... this is just market experience me talking" (11:12–11:44). "This is how I label it: just from the wick down to the start of the body" (11:46–11:49).
- Also: "price coming in touching just the body of the candle" (26:13–26:17).
- **Ambiguous:** *which* candle's wick. Gemini says TJR re-drew the box on the 1-hour S&P from "the wick high to the start of the candle body" of one candle, but its prices conflict with that (see the screen check). Screenshots needed.

### 3. Entry, targets, using it as a target (11:51–13:41)
- Price taps in, then "whether you want to wait for this break of structure right here, and then you can go long, target previous areas of liquidity" (11:53–12:10). "Maybe you get confirmation off of this break of structure right here, cool, enter" (13:06–13:13).
- A leg up can be a single candle: "where was the leg up prior to the break of structure, boom, this whole candle right here" (12:43–12:47).
- "I sometimes use order blocks as take profit" (13:39–13:41).

### 4. The whole chain, live on the chart (13:45–15:31)
- "Liquidity sweep, break of structure, perfect, this is an entry for a short ... we fill an imbalance, we fill this order block, we get a reaction ... up into this imbalance, we fill this imbalance, scale down ... target this liquidity all the way down here, takes it out. Then what happens: rallies, makes another order block."
- Price can return to the same block more than once: "comes back down into the order block again, boom, another rally" (14:45–14:53).
- "This should be like clockwork to you guys by now" (15:21–15:30).

### 5. Gold: order block, gap inside it, and patience (15:43–18:16)
- Sweep, big rally, break up, now "seeing some bearish pressure".
- "What are our retracement tools? **Fair value gap for really any retracement, order block off the first retracement, and equilibrium**, which we haven't gotten yet" (16:44–16:56).
- There's a huge fair value gap inside the order block: "either or" (16:11–16:20).
- Wait for price to fill and break structure → long; targets "previous areas of liquidity" (17:06–17:16).
- If it doesn't break structure and comes lower into the order block: "we wait for confirmation. What's our confirmation? A break of structure that shows market structure shift" (17:19–17:33).
- You can also skip that break and enter on the next retracement: "off of this order block or this fair value gap within here, off of a 15-minute break of structure ... stops wherever you want, and then you can target all the way up here for crazy RR" (17:41–18:02).

### 6. Bias and the timeframe cascade (18:48–22:16)
- GBP 1-hour: sweep, break, price "pokes its head" back in twice, break down → short; targets liquidity (18:54–19:34).
- "Long that thing, **especially if it's in line with our daily and our weekly bias**." A daily bias shift is the daily break of structure (20:23–20:36).
- **Daily order block, not enough alone:** price reaches the daily block; "this isn't enough, this is not enough in itself". Then a 4-hour break up; on the hourly "you know your bias is bullish, so you're waiting for any bullish confirmation": hourly break up, the hourly order block, price comes in, break of structure → long; targets liquidity (20:44–21:44).

### 7. When it's messy (22:27–25:10)
- No clear 1-hour break until "long gone": go to the 15-minute (22:27–22:51).
- "Kind of a big ass price range ... not much else you can do with that"; price is already in the leg when it breaks (23:12–23:31).
- Already inside the range at the break: "you can wait just for double confirmation", or wait for price to go a bit higher and retrace in, or use the imbalance inside it on the 15-minute (24:39–25:10).
- "If you see a liquidity sweep and then a leg higher, you should assume that there's going to be an order block there" (23:45–23:51).

### 8. Close (26:28–27:07)
- "You can't tell me this doesn't work ... it just takes practice, that market experience." Next trading day: **equilibrium**.
